A screen you can check, not a ruling you have to trust

Many people want to know whether a listed company's business and finances fit a Shariah screen, and why. This tool applies one published method to public SEC filings and market prices. For every verdict it keeps the ratios, their limits, the filing used and the data it could not read, so the result can be checked. It does not issue rulings.

How a verdict is made

Pass
Every input was available and every ratio is within its limit.
Fail
A business screen failed, or a financial ratio is over its limit.
Needs review
A borderline business case. It is never passed automatically. A reviewer may resolve it with a recorded override.
Insufficient data
A required input is missing or out of date. It is never treated as a pass.

The checks run in this order, and the first one that applies decides:

  1. The business screen fails: fail.
  2. A required input is missing or out of date: insufficient data.
  3. A financial ratio is over its limit: fail.
  4. The business case is borderline: needs review.
  5. Otherwise: pass.

Some checks can turn a pass into insufficient data. None of them changes a fail or a review.

How to read a tile

  • Size is spot market cap: the latest close times the reported share count.
  • Colour is the status. The tool can also colour tiles by the last-close price change, which is shown only when the export includes prices. Green and red mean the price moved up or down, and gray means it barely moved.
  • Outline or hatching marks a pass or a review within 10% of a limit, measured relative to the limit.
  • ! marks a balance sheet that is out of date. Lower-confidence interest bases and the other flags are listed in the audit record.
  • Click a tile for its audit record: each ratio with its limit and headroom, the filing used, and the XBRL facts behind it.

Method and data

Thresholds

RatioLimit
Interest-bearing debt / market capbelow 30%
Cash and interest-bearing securities / market capbelow 30%
Impure income (interest) / revenuebelow 5%

These limits come from secondary summaries of AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 21. They have not yet been checked against the AAOIFI text itself. The business rules and the market cap method are the project's own reading.

Data sources

  • Constituents: the Wikipedia list of S&P 500 companies.
  • Filings and financial facts: SEC EDGAR, its company facts, and each filing's own XBRL.
  • Prices: yfinance. It is an unofficial source and can be throttled or break. A run that cannot read every close is not published.

Point in time

A verdict for a date uses only filings filed on or before that date, and only closing prices from before it.

The full method, its known limitations and the open items are in the README.

Questions

Is this a ruling?

No. It applies published thresholds to public data and records how it reached each verdict. A ruling needs a qualified scholar, and this page does not replace one.

Have the thresholds been checked against the AAOIFI text?

Not yet. They come from secondary summaries of AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 21. The README lists the items still open.

Why does a company show insufficient data?

A required input is missing, or the balance sheet is out of date. That status is never treated as a pass.

What does a review override do?

A reviewer can resolve a needs-review case, with a recorded reason and date. The override expires after 365 days. It never changes a fail, and it never changes a pass.

How is market cap measured?

Closing price times reported shares. Verdicts use the 12-month average of daily market cap, and the spot value is shown alongside it.

Why are some price changes missing?

Prices come from yfinance, an unofficial source. The daily change is shown only when every company's closes could be read, so a partial set is never published.

Can I use this for investment decisions?

No. This is not investment advice. A pass says nothing about whether a company is a good investment.

What this is not

This is an automated screening and research aid. It is not a religious ruling (fatwa), not a certification that any company is permissible, and not investment advice.

The data can be late, incomplete or wrong. Consult a qualified scholar before relying on any result, and read the audit record for any company you care about.
